    How to read the columns

    Share of reviews. The dot is the estimate; the line is the 95% confidence interval. Where two intervals overlap a lot, the two issues may not truly differ.

    Rating gap. Mean stars of reviews that raise the issue minus mean stars of reviews that don't, with a 95% CI (Welch). Left of the line means lower ratings. This is a difference, not a cause. Hover it for mean, median, mode and SD.

    Across countries. Coefficient of variation (CV) of the issue's share across the countries. Under 30%: even. 30 to 60%: varies. Over 60%: concentrated in few countries.

    κ. Cohen's kappa between the main labeling pass and an independent second pass. Below 0.70 is flagged as less reliable.

    Each week's share is weighted by rating count across the countries with reviews that week. Apple returns only the newest 500 reviews per country, so early weeks hold fewer countries; hollow points mark weeks with fewer than 50 reviews. The earlier vs recent comparison is made within each country (Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test) and adjusted for the number of comparisons (Holm), so a change in country mix cannot create a false trend.

    Country differences

    Share of each country's reviews, per problem

    Chi-square test of independence between country and mention of the problem, adjusted for the number of tests (Holm). p < 0.05 means the shares are unlikely to be equal across countries. Cramér's V shows how strong the difference is: under 0.1 is negligible, 0.1 to 0.3 is small to moderate, over 0.3 is strong.
